Calendar feed

This profile's upcoming appointments, its expected lab draws and its medication reminders, in your own calendar app.

More about this

Sanguity sends nothing: your calendar app fetches a private address every few hours. The feed is yours alone and shows only what you can already see here. An expected lab draw is a guess from the steady rhythm of a panel's past draws, never an order or a booking. Medication reminders are the ones set on a medication's details (Timeline → Medications); each repeats at its times in the time zone it was set in.

What each event says

Calendars often sync to your phone maker's or email provider's cloud. Minimal keeps health details out of it. Neither ever includes results or diagnoses.

Your feed's address

Shown only now. Anyone with it can see what the feed shows, so keep it private; if it gets out, get a new address.

  • iPhone or iPad: Settings → Apps → Calendar → Calendar Accounts → Add Account → Other → Add Subscribed Calendar. Paste the address.
  • Mac (Calendar): File → New Calendar Subscription. Paste the address; set Auto-refresh to every hour or day.
  • Google Calendar: on the web, Other calendars → + → From URL. Paste the address. Google fetches it from its own servers, so this server must be reachable from the internet, and it refreshes only every several hours.
  • Outlook: Add calendar → Subscribe from web. Paste the address and give it a name.

Everyone's feeds of this profile

As an owner you can turn off anyone's feed. A feed also stops when its person's access to the profile ends.

One or two pages to print or bring to an appointment, made from the records you see here.

More about this

It can hold results out of range since the last visit, trends of your key labs, current medications, upcoming appointments and your own questions. It's made each time you ask, and isn't kept. Anyone the profile is shared with can make one.

    More about this

    It holds this profile's records, documents, settings and activity: to keep a copy of your own, or to move the profile to another server.

    FHIR R4 is the standard format health apps and patient portals read. A FHIR export holds the records you tick as one bundle, with the documents' files beside it in the zip. Sanguity's own import reads it back too.

    More about this

    A profile's owners and editors change these, and everyone it's shared with sees them. They're encrypted like the records and go with the profile's export. A share link shows them only if its owner ticks About this person.
