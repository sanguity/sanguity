Calendar feed

This profile's upcoming appointments, its expected lab draws and its medication reminders, in your own calendar app.

Nothing is sent unless you set up notifications to your own server: your calendar app fetches a private address every few hours. The feed is yours alone and shows only what you can already see here. An expected lab draw is a guess from the steady rhythm of a panel's past draws, never an order or a booking. Medication reminders are the ones set on a medication's details (the Medications tab); each repeats at its times in the time zone it was set in. The address stops working for good when you lose access to this profile, change your password, remove a passkey, sign out all other devices, sign in with a recovery code, or an admin resets your password or two-factor sign-in: make a new one then.

Your feed's address

Shown only now. Anyone with it can see what the feed shows, so keep it private; if it gets out, get a new address.

  • iPhone or iPad: Settings → Apps → Calendar → Calendar Accounts → Add Account → Other → Add Subscribed Calendar. Paste the address.
  • Mac (Calendar): File → New Calendar Subscription. Paste the address; set Auto-refresh to every hour or day.
  • Google Calendar: on the web, Other calendars → + → From URL. Paste the address. Google fetches it from its own servers, so this server must be reachable from the internet, and it refreshes only every several hours.
  • Outlook: Add calendar → Subscribe from web. Paste the address and give it a name.

    Emergency card

    A card to print and carry, and a short image for your phone's lock screen, made from the records.

    The card has the allergies with their reactions, current medications, active problems, blood type and emergency contacts, as of the day you make it. The lock-screen image has only the name, allergies, blood type and contacts.

    It's a file you keep: no one can open it from Sanguity without signing in, and no share link carries it. Make it again when something changes. Blood type and contacts are in Settings, under About this person.

    It says only what the records and your own entries say; it isn't medical advice.

    Export everything

    One zip of everything in this profile. Keep it as private as the records themselves.

    It holds this profile's records as they were imported, the corrections made to them, the journal with its photos, the medication log and reminders, documents and settings: to keep a copy of your own, or to move the profile to another server. The activity log is in it only when an owner exports.

    Uploaded on another Sanguity's import page, the records come back with their corrections (which can still be undone there), the journal and its photos, the medication log and reminders. Loaded by an admin with the command line (the README in the zip says how), the settings, watch data and earlier uploads come back too. Neither brings back the activity log, who the profile is shared with, share links, calendar feeds, connected sources and their sign-ins, the watch receiver's key, or which import each record came from: they arrive as one import.

    FHIR R4 is the standard format health apps and patient portals read. A FHIR export holds the records you tick as Sanguity shows them, with your corrections made, as one bundle, with the documents' files beside it in the zip. Sanguity's own import reads it back too.
